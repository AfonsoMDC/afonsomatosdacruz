Image, Doubt & Construction

A series of assignments around an exhibition at the S.M.A.K.

Type
School project
School
Don Bosco Sint-Denijs-Westrem, Ghent · 4D/A Graphic Techniques
When
Exhibition 21 April – 3 May 2026
Made
Poster, animation, archive box, photo editing, stop-motion, photography, algorithmic image, invitation and website

A series of assignments for an exhibition at the S.M.A.K. in Ghent, in which students entered into dialogue with the work of photographer Marc De Blieck. One question keeps returning: do you photograph what is there, or do you construct what the viewer sees? I explored it through a poster, animation, an object, stop-motion, an algorithmic image, print and a website.

01

Shadow and suggestion

How do you tell a story with only light and dark? Starting from Plato’s cave, I designed a poster for an exhibition at the S.M.A.K. that plays with what you can and cannot see. A lantern casts its shadow on the wall; the title ‘Umbra’ and the line ‘Light only shows him the way’ run along the diagonal of the image.

02

From poster to moving image

The same poster, now as a short animation. With light and shadows moving subtly, the graphic construction gets a rhythm of its own on screen.

03

Archive box

An archive box is more than packaging: it says something about the history inside it. I designed the box and its label with attention to layout and typography, so the object fits the context of a historic city archive.

04

An old photo, rebuilt

Images are often constructed. I edited an old photo from Ghent in Photoshop by adding contemporary elements. The technique changes what the photo means: what looked like archive becomes a new image.

05

The Real Behind The Perfect Picture

A short film in stop-motion and shadow play about social media and identity: the translation of reality into a constructed image.

06

Organic construction

Inspired by the photographic work of Marc De Blieck, I looked for structures on the border between recording and composing. The bare trees seem caught by chance, but every element is a choice: viewpoint, format, moment and what falls just outside the frame. Black and white strengthens the graphic character of the branches.

07

From algorithm to composition

This image did not start from a sketch, but from numbers. An online pixel tool generated a grid from a base colour; in Illustrator I took colour swatches from the pattern and combined them with a generated circle structure. Then the designing began: moving circles, scaling shapes, leaving emptiness in place. The algorithm supplied the material, I made the composition. The square image measures 15×15 cm, on a portrait format of 20×32 cm set by the Golden Ratio.

08

Poster for the exhibition

The poster for the 4D/A Graphic Techniques exhibition at the S.M.A.K. The title is set in a serif, with one coloured letter per word; the pixel image carries the page.

09

Invitation

The final step: translating the pixel structures into a tangible product. The front repeats the title and image; the back invites guests to the exhibition and to the reception on Saturday 2 May at 3 p.m.

10

Website

All assignments of the project on one long page, each with a heading, a short explanation and its image. Built with HTML and CSS and shown here as a screenshot.
